A few things would make that top gallery feel much more premium and easier to use.

Right now the image is doing the job of a hero, but the gallery itself feels hidden. It reads more like a dark banner with arrows than a property photo experience.

What I would change:

1. Make the first image brighter and crisper
The current dark overlay is too heavy, so the house loses detail. Keep some gradient for text legibility, but lighten it a lot. Let the property sell itself.

2. Separate “hero image” from “gallery controls”
At the moment everything blends together. Treat it like:

big main photo
small thumbnail rail or photo counter
clean navigation controls

That instantly makes it feel intentional.

3. Add a thumbnail strip
On desktop, put 4 to 6 thumbnails along the bottom of the hero image or in a vertical stack on the right.
That gives users immediate proof there are real photos and lets them jump around without clicking arrows.

Best setup:

main large image
bottom thumbnail row with subtle border on active image
“+4 more” on last thumbnail if there are extra photos

4. Improve the arrows
The arrows feel a little generic and float too loosely. Make them:

smaller
on soft dark glass circles
vertically centered within the image only
appear stronger on hover

That makes the interaction feel tighter.

5. Move the “View all 8 photos” into a stronger position
That button is too disconnected on the right. Put it inside the image area, top right or bottom right, as a glass button. Example:

View all photos (8)

This should open a full screen lightbox.

6. Use a better crop
The current photo crop cuts the house in a way that feels slightly awkward. Try to ensure the first image shows:

full front elevation
entry if possible
a more balanced composition

The first image should feel like a listing cover, not just a random gallery frame.

7. Add a proper lightbox / fullscreen mode
When clicked, the gallery should open into a polished viewer with:

larger photo
thumbnails underneath
keyboard arrow support
swipe on mobile
photo count like 1 / 8

That alone would make the site feel much more high end.

8. Tighten the text overlap
Your address and badges are sitting pretty low and disconnected from the image. I would anchor the title area more intentionally in the lower left with better spacing, so it feels like part of the hero instead of floating under it.

9. Consider a split layout on desktop
A strong 2026 style pattern would be:

left: large featured image
right: stacked mini images or stat cards

That would work especially well since you already have the price/equity/ARV cards on the right. The whole top could become more editorial and less like a dark slider.

10. Make it feel more like a real estate product
Take cues from Airbnb, Zillow luxury listings, and modern developer sites:

less black haze
sharper image presentation
cleaner corners
visible photo navigation
stronger sense of image hierarchy

My recommended layout for your page:

Desktop

full width hero image, around 620 to 700px tall
bottom thumbnail strip
top right View all photos (8)
bottom left property title and location
right side stacked stat cards remain, but align cleaner to image height

Mobile

swipeable image
photo count pill in corner
thumbnails optional, or just dots
tap opens fullscreen gallery

If I were prioritizing the biggest wins first, I would do these 3:

lighten the overlay
add thumbnails
build a fullscreen lightbox

Those three changes would dramatically upgrade the section.
